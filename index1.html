About

Still learning, already building

I'm Yawan Sinjali Magar, known as Yawanjung — a self-taught, independent developer from Nepal. My interest in programming started after playing audio games and wondering how they were actually made. That curiosity led me to Python, and from there into web development, software, Android apps, and game development.

I didn't have a personal mentor guiding me through it. I learned largely through self-study, experimentation, documentation, online resources, and modern AI-assisted learning — and it wasn't always easy. I now have a solid grasp of programming fundamentals and practical Python, including Object-Oriented Programming, while I keep working on more advanced concepts.

I don't present myself as someone who has mastered everything. My focus is continuous improvement — becoming better than I was yesterday, adapting to new tools, and turning ideas into things that actually work.

Approach

Technology never stays in one place

It evolves with time. Understanding technology isn't about learning one fixed method and staying there forever — it takes continuously adapting, experimenting, and upgrading the way you work. I try to judge technology by the problems it solves and the value it provides, not by how advanced or impressive it looks.

My approach is problem first, technology second: understand the problem, think about what would actually help, then choose the tool that fits. Even a small, repeated annoyance is a problem worth solving — part of why ZenTools exists.

Accessibility

Built to include, not exclude

I'm blind myself, and that experience strongly shapes how I think about technology. I believe technology should be usable by everyone, and that accessibility should be considered from the start of development — not added at the end as a checkbox. Technology should make possibilities wider, not narrower.

This thinking runs through projects like A Separate World, an audio-first game built with blind and low-vision players in mind.
